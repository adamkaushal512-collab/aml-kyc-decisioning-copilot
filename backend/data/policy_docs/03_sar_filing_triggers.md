# Policy AML-032: Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) Filing Triggers

**Scope:** Applies to all cases arising from transaction monitoring alerts,
KYC refresh findings, and escalated sanctions/PEP matches where potential
suspicious activity is identified.

## Clauses

1. If a transaction or pattern of transactions has no apparent lawful
   business or economic purpose after reasonable investigation, a SAR must
   be filed regardless of the transaction amount.

2. If a confirmed sanctions match (per Policy AML-014) is validated as a
   true positive, a SAR must be filed in addition to any required blocking
   or rejection of the transaction.

3. If a customer structures transactions to fall below reporting or
   monitoring thresholds (see Policy AML-045), and no reasonable business
   explanation is provided, a SAR must be filed.

4. A SAR must be filed no later than 30 calendar days from the date of
   initial detection of the suspicious activity; if no suspect can be
   identified, the filing deadline may be extended to 60 calendar days.

5. If the analyst is uncertain whether activity meets the SAR filing
   threshold, the case must be escalated to a senior compliance reviewer
   rather than cleared without a documented second opinion.

6. A decision not to file a SAR must be documented with specific rationale
   tied to the evidence reviewed; "insufficient information" alone is not
   an acceptable rationale if further investigation was reasonably
   available.

7. All SAR filing decisions, filed or not filed, must be retained in the
   permanent audit record along with the supporting evidence considered.
